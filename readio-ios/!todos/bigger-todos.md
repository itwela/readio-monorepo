HOW TO MEDITATE –

“Before the mind can be clear, it must first be still.”

Meditation is not about stopping your thoughts—it’s about learning to sit with them. It’s not about clearing the mind, it’s about gaining clarity within the mind. Like a bucket of muddy water set down on a table—if you stop stirring, the dirt settles, and the water becomes clear on its own. The mind works the same way—clarity comes not from forcing thoughts away, but from allowing them to settle on their own. 

And from that stillness; the mind is transformed from a muddy mirage of constant chatter; to a reflective mirror of clarity and truth. 

And that’s just the beginning…

Across cultures and centuries, seekers have followed this path—recognizing that true peace is not found in forcing control of external factors, but in cultivating the power of internal focus.

The Tao Te Ching asks: “Do you have the patience to wait until your mud settles and the water is clear?”

 The Buddha taught: “Do not dwell in the past, do not dream of the future, concentrate the mind on the present moment.” 

—Meditation is not an escape—it is the art of presence. It is the practice of returning to the here and now, again and again.

🕊 The Bible teaches: “In returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and trust shall be your strength.” (Isaiah 30:15)

—Stillness is not weakness—it is where true strength is found. A heart at rest can hear wisdom more clearly.

No matter the tradition, the message remains the same: peace is not something we chase—it is something we learn to choose.

•

How to Meditate: The Simple Practice

There are many different pathways and styles to explore meditation. 

However, here are 5 basic points to get you started .

Find a Comfortable Seat – Sit on the floor, on a cushion, or in a chair. Keep your back straight but relaxed. No need to force a posture—just be at ease.

Close Your Eyes & Breathe – Inhale deeply through your nose. Exhale slowly through your nose. Let the breath be your anchor.

Observe, and Return – Thoughts will come. Let them pass like clouds. The practice is not about trying to stop thinking—the aim is simply to improve your skill of noticing the thoughts as they arrive while developing the subtle ability to not let them carry your attention with them,  by gently returning your focus back to your breath again and again. That’s the practice. When your mind wanders (and it will), gently return to your breath. Each return is the practice.
There’s no shortcut. No secret technique. 

Like any other worthy skill, Presence is a skill developed with intentional practice. 

We don’t need to be perfect. We don’t need to try and  forcefully silence the mind. We just need to show up—consistently and let stillness do the rest.

Now, let this truth settle as we begin meditation.
